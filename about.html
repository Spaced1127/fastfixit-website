Most repairs start with the same question: is it the part everyone replaces, or the thing feeding it? We test the path before we quote.

A console with no picture is not always a bad HDMI port. A controller with drift is not always a bad stick. That takes longer than swapping assemblies. It also costs you less, and it's the only way to know the fault is actually gone.

We work on the things people keep: a launch PS5, a Game Boy that belonged to someone's brother, a laptop with every photo on it. The data on a device is yours, not ours.

If we can't fix it, you'll get a straight explanation and the device back. If we can, you'll get a written quote before we start. No approval, no work.

How we work.

Three rules, in order. They make the schedule longer than a board swap and the invoice shorter than a guess.

Measure first.

Bench supply, meter and thermal camera before the parts drawer. A fault you can measure is a fault you can fix.

Quote first.

You get the likely cause, the part price and the labour in writing. We don't order a part until you approve it.

Test last.

Power, video, audio, inputs, wireless and thermals. Then a soak test with a real workload. If it fails, it stays on the bench.

What's on the bench.

The gear that makes board-level work possible, and the reason a port repair doesn't start with a whole new board.
